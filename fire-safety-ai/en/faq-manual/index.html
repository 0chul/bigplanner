How to write an approved FAQ answer

  1. Choose one recurring question

    Group questions that ask for the same outcome. Start with a routine request such as visitor parking, delivery hours or where to submit a maintenance issue. State which building the answer applies to.

  2. Write from the current building policy

    Use the approved source to write a short answer that gives the tenant a clear next step. Record the source title and revision, the approving role and the next review date. Have that role approve the answer for use.

  3. Define when staff should take over

    Name who handles missing information, requests for approval and situations outside policy. Specify the context the receiving team needs, such as the building and the question. For emergencies, use the building’s existing emergency channels.

  4. Test different ways tenants ask

    Check the standard question and a variation such as “Where can my guest park?” Both should give the same approved answer. Test an unclear question and one outside policy, such as a weekend parking request, to verify that the helpdesk asks for clarification or routes it to staff.

Fictional example for visitor parking

Building A, its policy and the dates below are fictional.

Tenant question
How do visitors arrange parking?
Example answer
At Building A, the tenant hosting the visit registers the guest with reception through the tenant portal before arrival. Reception confirms availability and any charges under the current visitor parking policy.
Policy source
Use this answer for standard visits at Building A. Source is the Visitor Parking Policy, revision 2, dated 20 November 2026. The property manager approves the answer. Next review is 20 December 2026.
When to hand off
Send same-day exceptions, weekend requests and questions about parking fees or validation to reception. Reception checks the current policy and confirms the arrangements.

Review before publishing or updating

  • Have someone who did not write the entry check that each instruction follows the approved source.
  • Confirm the answer applies to the named building, visit type and operating hours.
  • Check that staff receiving an exception can see the question, the location and the next action.
  • Review affected entries when the building policy changes and replace outdated answers or withdraw them.
