Six checks before you start

Review recent records. Pick two or three request types with enough examples to test, such as visitor parking, weak cooling and after-hours HVAC. Record the building, hours, request types and pilot owner.

For routine questions, record the approved manual section and who updates it. Test one answer against the latest wording. For site visits, capture the location and issue. Authorised staff review and decide requests that need approval.

Identify who reviews new requests, assigns follow-up and handles cases outside the pilot. Walk through one unassigned request at shift change to confirm who picks it up.

Record the ticket ID, location, issue, assignment, status, next action and check-in time. Ask the incoming shift to record when it reviewed the case.

Choose the authoritative system. Confirm which phone, email and work-order integrations the pilot supports. Test one issue reported twice to see how staff will reconcile the records.

Agree who can view or edit tenant information. Decide when contact details or photos are needed, how long to retain them and who handles deletion. Use fictional or redacted records for the walkthrough.

For emergencies, use the building’s established emergency contacts and response procedures.

Compare a small baseline with the pilot

Decide in advance what would justify continuing. For example, compare five working days before the pilot with five during it. Match weekdays, day and night shift windows, and request types. Count duplicate reports once using the ticket ID or the time, location and issue.

  • Record completeness. Divide cases with the location, issue and assignment status recorded by all in-scope cases that needed a site check.
  • Handover acknowledgement. Divide open cases whose first handover includes a timestamped check of the status and next action by all open cases that needed handover.

Record both counts for each measure. If no cases qualify, record 0 of 0 and leave the rate blank. Review missing records and delayed assignments with both shifts before deciding whether to expand.
